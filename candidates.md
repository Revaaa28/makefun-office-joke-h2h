# Candidates — The Funniest Office Joke, Judged Head to Head

## Judge brief (in order)

1. One-line office joke with a setup and a turn.
2. Built on a universal office truth.
3. Surprise — not a plain complaint.
4. One line, with the punchline last.
5. Fresh — not a "meeting could have been an email" variant.

Gate: `entry.txt` must be 1–200 characters, single line.

## Anticipated strong rivals

- "This meeting could have been an email," played plain — the most
  familiar office joke in existence; judges have seen it a thousand
  times, and the brief explicitly rules out leaning on that frame.
- A plain printer-jam joke — universally recognised, but the premise is
  a cliché and most versions are complaints rather than jokes with a
  turn.
- A plain reply-all joke — another well-worn office premise; without a
  genuinely unexpected twist it reads as an observation, not a joke.

## CANDIDATE A — CHOSEN

> I told my manager I was blocked, and she unblocked me by adding me to a meeting about being blocked.

Rationale: built on the universal truth that being blocked spawns a
meeting about being blocked. The turn is unexpected — the manager's
"help" is the problem wearing a fix's clothes — and the punchline
("a meeting about being blocked") lands last. It is fresh: it never
touches the "meeting could have been an email" frame.

## CANDIDATE B

> The printer only jams when the document matters. I have started printing blank pages just to watch it work perfectly out of spite.

Loses: the printer-jam premise is more cliché than A's blocked/meeting
loop, so it starts from a weaker, more familiar place against the
freshness criterion.

## CANDIDATE C

> My boss asked for a quick sync, so I booked a meeting to discuss whether we need the meeting, and now there are two meetings.

Loses: the "now there are two meetings" punchline is weaker than A's
absurd logic — A's ending names the exact self-referential loop, while
C's ending is a predictable escalation.

## Selection note

Selection was MANUAL — no Gemini judge, no NVIDIA-based judging, and no
API judge of any kind was used (Rev's rule, 2026-11-24). Candidates were
ranked by hand against the judge brief above.
